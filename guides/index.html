Cape Town solar, explained without the jargon

Registration, batteries, feed-in, tax and more. Reviewed October 2026. Rules and tariffs change, so confirm key points with the City of Cape Town or your installer.

Registering with the City of Cape Town

All solar and battery systems connected to the City of Cape Town grid must be registered and authorised as Small-Scale Embedded Generation (SSEG).

  • Your installer normally submits the application and uses a City-approved inverter.
  • Systems that export need a bi-directional meter and reverse power flow protection.
  • You need to be on the Home User tariff to register solar.
  • Ask for the electrical Certificate of Compliance when the work is finished.

Unregistered systems can lead to fines and problems with insurance or selling your home, so do not skip it.

Grid-tied, hybrid or off-grid

Grid-tied systems have no battery. They cut your bill during the day but switch off when the grid does. Hybrid systems add a battery, so selected circuits stay on during an outage and you can use stored energy in the evening. Off-grid systems disconnect entirely and are uncommon in suburbs because they need much larger batteries.

Most Cape Town homes that want backup choose hybrid. See what each costs.

Batteries explained

  • kWh is how much energy the battery stores. kW is how much it can deliver at once. You need both to be big enough for the appliances you want to run.
  • Most new home batteries use lithium iron phosphate (LFP) chemistry. Check the cycle count and warranty.
  • Size the battery to the load you want to back up, not to the biggest one on the market.
  • Heavy loads such as geysers, ovens and pool pumps drain a battery quickly. Consider a solar geyser or timer.

Selling power back to the City

The City publishes a residential feed-in tariff each year in its budget. The 2026/27 annexure lists about 107.98 c/kWh excluding VAT, and rates are reviewed annually. To be paid for exports you need a bi-directional meter. Many homes use most of the power they generate, so export income is often a small part of the saving.

Solar and power cuts

Eskom has not load shed nationally since 16 May 2025. Localised load reduction and other outages can still occur.

A grid-tied system without a battery gives no power during a cut. If backup matters, you need a hybrid system, and you should decide whether it needs to run essentials only or the whole house.

Tax and incentives

There is currently no income-tax rebate for rooftop solar on a primary residence. The Section 12B allowance applies to businesses. Older guides still mention a 25% rebate (up to R15 000) and the 125% business allowance. Both have ended. Check the SARS website or ask an accountant, especially if you run a business.

Renting or sectional title

Tenants need their landlord's written approval and usually shouldn't invest in a fixed system. In sectional title schemes the body corporate generally must approve any rooftop installation, and roofs are often shared, so ask whether a shared system is possible.

Looking after your system

  • Check the monitoring app now and then for faults or drops in output.
  • Clean panels if dust or bird droppings build up, using water and a soft brush, from the ground where possible.
  • Keep records: the Certificate of Compliance, registration and warranties.
  • Have a technician inspect the system if production drops without explanation.

Is there a tax rebate for home solar?

Is there still load shedding in Cape Town?

Eskom has not load shed nationally since 16 May 2025. Localised load reduction and other outages can still occur.

Can I be paid for exporting power?

The City publishes a residential feed-in tariff in its annual budget. The 2026/27 annexure lists about 107.98 c/kWh excluding VAT. Rates are reviewed each year, so confirm the current rate on the City's website.

Hybrid or grid-tied?

Grid-tied systems have no battery and switch off during an outage. Hybrid systems add a battery so selected circuits stay on. Hybrids cost more but give backup.

How long does approval take?

Secondary guides suggest roughly 2–4 weeks for City authorisation. Ask your installer for their current experience.
